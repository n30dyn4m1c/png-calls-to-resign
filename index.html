Do calls to resign work in Papua New Guinea?

Told to Quit.
Almost No One Does.

27 public calls · 15 people · 1 resignation

Since 2019, PNG leaders have stood up at least 27 times and said someone should resign. We went back through the news to find out what happened each time. Here's the short version: one person resigned.

Quick vocab check

PNG politics uses these words loosely. In this story they mean four different things.

Resign
You give up the job for good, by your own decision. There's no way back except being appointed again from scratch.
Step aside
You stop doing the job for a while, usually during an investigation or court case. Someone acts in your place. A minister keeps their seat in Parliament and can be put back.
Removed
Someone above you takes the job away: the PM in a reshuffle or sacking, or a board. You didn't choose to go.
Suspended
Like stepping aside, but imposed on you by an employer or board. In this record it happened once, to Wapu Sonk at Kumul Petroleum, before the board terminated him. It has no mark of its own on the charts.

Here's every call we found

May 2019

Peter O'Neill actually resigned

1 person

Opposition Leader Patrick Pruaitch told him to go on 3 May. James Marape said it again on 27 May, after walking out of government with a group of ministers. Two days later O'Neill was gone.

That's the only real resignation in seven years of calls. And by then O'Neill had lost his majority, so a no-confidence vote would probably have finished him anyway.

2023 to 2026

Three ministers stepped aside. That's different.

3

Justin Tkatchenko called his critics primitive animals. Jimmy Maladina was charged with assault in Sydney. The charges were later dropped. Billy Joseph got caught up in an army recruitment row. Each stepped aside within two days of being told to.

Stepping aside is a pause. The minister hands the portfolio to someone else, keeps their seat in Parliament, and can be put back. Two of the three came back: Maladina was reinstated after three months and Tkatchenko after eight. Only Joseph's pause became permanent, when the PM took over Defence.

2024 to 2026

Five more were moved by their boss

5

Ian Ling-Stuckey was demoted from Treasurer two days after Sir Julius Chan asked the PM to sack him. Peter Tsiamalili went from Police to Works. Win Daki said no to stepping aside, then lost Commerce in a reshuffle anyway. Joseph lost Defence a few weeks after his step-aside.

The Kumul Petroleum board terminated Wapu Sonk two months after the PM told him to step aside. None of these five chose to go. Someone with the power to move them did it.

Most of the time

And the rest? Nothing happened.

15 of 26

The police commissioner. The Deputy PM. The Mining Minister. An MP charged with murder, who kept his seat and was later acquitted. A health secretary the doctors' association didn't want.

They were all told to go. They all stayed.

2020 to 2025

James Marape has heard it six times

0 for 6

Opposition leaders, his own MPs after the 2024 riots and O'Neill himself have all told him to quit. He's still Prime Minister.

Who gets told to go

If you're a minister, worry a little

8 of 11

Ministers are the only group where a call tends to end with someone out of a job. Usually that means the PM reshuffled them. None of them resigned.

MPs and public servants? We found five calls aimed at them. None worked.

Why they were told to go

Scandals land harder than politics

6 of 9

When the call was about something the person did (an offensive remark, a criminal charge, corruption allegations), they ended up out of the post six times out of nine.

When it was about politics or performance, like crime, riots or a budget fight, it worked 5 times out of 17.

Who made the call

Friendly fire hits harder

3 of 5

Government MPs called for a resignation only five times, and three of those landed, including O'Neill in 2019. Opposition MPs made 17 calls. Seven landed.

The PM made three calls himself. The one on Sonk worked, the one on O'Neill in 2025 didn't, and the one on the UPNG chancellor is still open.

From call to exit

Exits happen fast, or not at all

6 of 9

Of the nine exits, four came within two days of the first call and six within three weeks.

The slow ones took a month or more. O'Neill held on for 26 days, Joseph lost Defence after six weeks, and Sonk lasted 69 days.

How often

And the calls keep coming

8 in 2025

Between 2020 and 2023 we found one or two a year. Since the January 2024 riots it's been more like one every two months.

Older calls are harder to find online, so the early years are probably undercounted.

James Nomane

One MP made a quarter of them

7 of 27

Chuave MP James Nomane made seven of the calls, first as a government MP who quit after the riots and later as Opposition Leader.

Two of his targets lost their posts. Both times, the PM moved them.

Elsewhere

How does PNG compare?

8%

Count only real resignations and 2 of the 26 calls worked, both about O'Neill. In Germany, researchers counted 111 public fights over a minister's future between 1969 and 2005. Fourteen ended in a resignation, about 13%.

In Britain, about half the ministers who faced a call between 1945 and 1997 eventually left, though it took almost two years on average. Nobody keeps a global average, because every study counts calls a little differently.

2 November 2026

The newest one is a day old

After UPNG's Vice-Chancellor quit citing bullying, Marape told Chancellor Sir Robert Igara to resign forthwith. We'll update this when we know how it ends.

So, do they work?

Mostly, no

6 of 26

Six calls got the person to resign or step aside, and five of those were step-asides. Five more ended with a boss moving someone. The other 15 went nowhere.

In PNG, telling someone to resign gets you a headline. Whether they actually leave comes down to the Prime Minister and the numbers in Parliament.

The four who did what they were told

Every person in our record who resigned or stepped aside after being asked to. Only one of them actually resigned.

  1. ResignedPeter O'Neill, Prime Minister. Resigned 29 May 2019, after calls from Opposition Leader Patrick Pruaitch (3 May) and James Marape (27 May). Gave up the office for good. The only resignation in the record.
  2. Stepped asideJustin Tkatchenko, Foreign Minister. Stepped aside 12 May 2023, a day after calls from Joseph Lelang and Belden Namah. Back in the job eight months later.
  3. Stepped asideJimmy Maladina, Petroleum Minister. Stepped aside 10 July 2024, two days after a call from Douglas Tomuriesa. Reinstated three months later, on 8 October 2024. Charges dropped in 2025.
  4. Stepped asideBilly Joseph, Defence Minister. Stepped aside 9 April 2026, a day after a call from Peter O'Neill. The PM took over Defence in May, so the pause became permanent.

How this was put together

  • Sources: The National, PNG Today, OnePNG, EMTV, NBC PNG, The PNG Bulletin, PNG Haus Bung, RNZ Pacific, ABC, AP and PINA, searched in November 2026.
  • A "call" is a named public figure or organisation publicly asking a named office-holder to resign, step down, step aside, be sacked or have their appointment revoked. Joint statements naming two targets count once per target.
  • Callers are grouped as Prime Minister, government MP, Opposition MP (by their side at the time of the call; governors count as MPs) or civil society (professional bodies and associations). Reasons are grouped as personal conduct (the person's own alleged words or actions) or politics or performance (leadership, crime, budgets, management). Targets are grouped as Prime Minister, Minister, MP, public servant (police commissioner, department heads) or SOE or statutory head (state company bosses, university chancellor).
  • "Resigned" means the person gave up the office for good. "Stepped aside" means a temporary handover of the job, which can be reversed; we count it separately. "Removed" means the person did not quit but lost the post when the PM, Cabinet or a board sacked or reshuffled them.
  • Departure dates are the day the person stepped aside, resigned or was replaced. "Days to exit" counts from the call to that date.
  • Comparisons: Germany from Fischer, Kaiser and Rohlfing, "The Push and Pull of Ministerial Resignations in Germany, 1969 to 2005" (West European Politics, 2006). UK from Berlinski, Dewan and Dowding, "Individual and Collective Performance and the Tenure of British Ministers 1945 to 1997", which records 225 resignation calls against 158 ministers; the share who eventually left is our calculation from its figures. These studies define a call more broadly than we do, so the comparison is a rough guide.
  • Allegations are described as they were reported at the time. Being told to resign is not proof of wrongdoing. Lohia Boe Samuel was acquitted of murder in 2023, Jimmy Maladina's charges were dropped in 2025, and Wapu Sonk denies the allegations against him. In our searches we found no conviction of anyone named here over the matters behind these calls, though we did not check every court record.
  • A step-aside, sacking or reshuffle that followed a call may have had other causes. We record the sequence, not the cause.
  • This is not a complete record. Many calls in print and on radio never reach online archives, so treat counts as a floor. Outcomes for David Manning (2026) and Rainbo Paita rest on finding no report that they left.
