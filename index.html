In Papua New Guinea, high-level figures rarely resign when they are called out.

One resignation in seven years of public calls.

By Neo Malesa · Since May 2019 · 27 public calls · 15 people

Since May 2019, senior office-holders in PNG have been publicly told to resign at least 27 times. The 15 people on the receiving end include two prime ministers, eight Cabinet ministers, the police commissioner, a department head, the head of the state oil company, a university chancellor and an MP. We went back through the news to see what happened to each of them.

Three terms

PNG politics uses these words loosely. In this story each one means something specific, and each has its own mark on the charts.

Resign
You give up the job for good, by your own decision. There's no way back except being appointed again from scratch.
Step aside
You stop doing the job for a while, usually during an investigation or court case. Someone acts in your place. A minister keeps their seat in Parliament and can be put back.
Moved or sacked
Someone above you takes the job away: the PM in a reshuffle or sacking, or a board. You didn't choose to go.

A black dot means the person stayed. When we say someone left, we mean any of the three. Wapu Sonk was suspended by the Kumul Petroleum board before it terminated him; we count that as sacked.

May 2019

Peter O'Neill resigned

1 of 15 people
  1. 3 MayOpposition Leader Patrick Pruaitch tells him to step down.
  2. 26 MayMinisters walk out. O'Neill says he will step down and hand over to Sir Julius Chan.
  3. 27 MayJames Marape, leading the breakaway, says that is not enough and that he must formally resign to the Governor-General.
  4. 29 MayO'Neill hands in his resignation.
  5. 30 MayParliament elects Marape.

It is the only resignation in seven years of calls, and by then O'Neill had lost his majority. A no-confidence vote would probably have removed him anyway.

2023 to 2026

Four people stepped aside

4 people

Justin Tkatchenko called his critics primitive animals. Jimmy Maladina was charged with assault in Sydney; the charges were later dropped. Billy Joseph was caught up in an army recruitment row. On 3 November 2026, a day after the Prime Minister told him to resign, UPNG Chancellor Sir Robert Igara stepped aside while the university council dealt with its leadership crisis. All four stepped aside within two days of the call.

Stepping aside is temporary. The minister hands the portfolio to someone else, keeps their seat in Parliament and can be reappointed. All three ministers were back in Cabinet within eight months: Maladina returned to Petroleum after three months and Tkatchenko to Foreign Affairs after eight. Joseph did not get Defence back, but in July the Prime Minister made him Health Minister.

2024 to 2026

Four more were moved or sacked

4 more people

Ian Ling-Stuckey lost the Treasury two days after Sir Julius Chan asked the Prime Minister to sack him. Peter Tsiamalili Jr was moved from Police to Works. Win Bakri Daki refused to step aside, then lost Commerce in the July 2026 reshuffle. The Kumul Petroleum board sacked Wapu Sonk two months after the Prime Minister told him to step aside.

None of them chose to go. Ministers were the most likely to lose their jobs: six of the eight told to go lost their portfolio, at least for a time. None resigned.

From call to leaving

Most departures came within days

7 of 9 people

Nine people left after a call. Five went within two days and seven within three weeks.

The two slow cases were O'Neill, who lasted 26 days after the Opposition's first call, and Sonk, whose board sacked him 69 days after the Prime Minister's call.

Most of the time

Most calls changed nothing

16 of 27 calls

The police commissioner, the Deputy Prime Minister and the Mining Minister all stayed. So did an MP charged with murder, who was later acquitted, and a health secretary the doctors' association opposed.

Billy Joseph is on this list too. A week after he stepped aside, Opposition MP Keith Iduhu said that was not enough: A minister must either resign properly, be dismissed, or face a vote of no confidence. None of those happened, and three months later Joseph was Health Minister.

2020 to 2025

James Marape has been told to go six times

0 of 6 calls

The calls came from Opposition leaders, from his own MPs after the January 2024 riots and from O'Neill. Nomane called him indecisive and weak. O'Neill said the country was in dire straits.

In March 2025 Marape replied: The job of a Prime Minister is not to resign at every crisis. He is still Prime Minister.

Seven years of calls

PNG's leaders rarely resign

1 of 15 people

One person resigned. Four stepped aside for a while, and all three ministers among them were back in Cabinet within eight months. Four more were moved or sacked by the Prime Minister or a board. The other six stayed in the job they were told to leave.

Over seven years and 27 calls, one person resigned. In PNG, high-level figures rarely resign when they are called out.

How this was put together

  • Sources: The National, PNG Today, OnePNG, EMTV, NBC PNG, The PNG Bulletin, PNG Haus Bung, RNZ Pacific, ABC, AP and PINA, searched in November 2026.
  • A "call" is a named public figure or organisation publicly asking a named office-holder to resign, step down, step aside, be sacked or have their appointment revoked. Joint statements naming two targets count once per target.
  • Callers are grouped as Prime Minister, government MP, Opposition MP (by their side at the time of the call; governors count as MPs) or civil society (professional bodies and associations). Reasons are grouped as personal conduct (the person's own alleged words or actions) or politics or performance (leadership, crime, budgets, management). Targets are grouped as Prime Minister, Minister, MP, public servant (police commissioner, department heads) or SOE or statutory head (state company bosses, university chancellor).
  • "Resigned" means the person gave up the office for good. "Stepped aside" means a temporary handover of the job, which can be reversed; we count it separately. "Moved or sacked" means the person did not quit but lost the post when the PM, Cabinet or a board sacked or reshuffled them. "Left" covers all three.
  • We count two ways and say which: people (15 people were told to quit) and calls (27 calls, because some people were told more than once). Headline figures are people. The charts count calls. Where groups are small (fewer than 10 calls), treat the pattern as a hint, not a finding.
  • Departure dates are the day the person stepped aside, resigned or was replaced. "Days from the call" counts from the call to that date.
  • Allegations are described as they were reported at the time. Being told to resign is not proof of wrongdoing. Lohia Boe Samuel was acquitted of murder in 2023, Jimmy Maladina's charges were dropped in 2025, and Wapu Sonk denies the allegations against him. In our searches we found no conviction of anyone named here over the matters behind these calls, though we did not check every court record.
  • A step-aside, sacking or reshuffle that followed a call may have had other causes. We record the sequence, not the cause.
  • This is not a complete record. Many calls in print and on radio never reach online archives, so treat counts as a floor. Rainbo Paita's outcome rests on finding no report that he left.
